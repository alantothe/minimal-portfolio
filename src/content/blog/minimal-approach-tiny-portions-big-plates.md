# A Minimal Approach : Tiny Portions on Big Plates

When I was building my portfolio site, I kept thinking about high-end restaurants. You know how they serve tiny portions on huge plates? That empty space around the food somehow makes it feel more valuable, more intentional. I wanted that same effect for my site.

## The Attention Budget

Here's something I've noticed: every element on a page asks for attention. Buttons, headlines, images—they all compete. When I was wireframing, I realized I could either show everything at once, or I could let a few things actually stand out.

I went with the second option.

## Space as a Design Choice

Surrounding elements with whitespace makes them impossible to ignore. It's not the only way to design—plenty of sites work great with dense layouts—but for a portfolio, I wanted that focused, deliberate feeling.

Dark background helped too. On black, everything you add creates visual weight. You can't really hide. Each element either earns its spot or it doesn't.

## The Restaurant Principle

Back to that restaurant idea: when content sits in a sea of empty space, it feels more important. More considered. I'm not saying every site should work this way—a news site or dashboard needs density. But for showing off a few projects and some basic info about myself? Space made sense.

It's like saying "this is all you need to see right now" instead of "here's everything I could possibly show you."

## Fighting the Urge to Fill

The tough part was leaving sections empty. I kept thinking "should I add more stats? More links? Another section?" But adding more would've defeated the point.

Sometimes less information communicates better than more. Not always, but for what I was trying to do—show I can build clean, focused interfaces—the minimal approach worked.

## Why This Approach Fit

I wanted my portfolio to feel calm and confident, not busy. The negative space creates that vibe without needing animations or flashy effects. It's quiet and direct.

This definitely isn't the only valid design approach. Content-heavy sites, e-commerce platforms, dashboards—they all need different strategies. But for a simple portfolio where the goal is "here's who I am and what I do," stripping things down and letting space do the work felt right.

Negative space isn't a rule. It's just one tool, and for this project, it was the right one.
